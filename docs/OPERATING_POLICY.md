# Operating Policy

## Objective

Maximize risk-adjusted, recurring digital-product profit per hour of owner involvement, while building a transferable business asset.

## Decision hierarchy

1. Protect legal/IP ownership and platform compliance.
2. Protect downside: reject opportunities with unacceptable risk or economics.
3. Validate demand with multiple independent signals where practical.
4. Prefer products that can be created once and delivered repeatedly.
5. Prefer low-support products with clear buyer outcomes.
6. Prefer higher-value differentiated products over commodity low-price printables.
7. Expand only after measured evidence supports expansion.

## Autonomy levels

### Autonomous

The engine may research, score, compare, document, test hypotheses, prepare product specifications, improve metadata, analyze performance, and recommend portfolio actions without owner approval.

### Owner approval required

The engine must stop before actions that require account authentication, payment authorization, material advertising spend, contractual commitments, uncertain intellectual-property rights, regulated/high-stakes claims, or publication when the connected system does not already authorize publication.

## Product creation policy

Original products are the default. Sourcing is allowed only when commercial/resale rights are explicit, verifiable, and documented. Questionable PLR/MRR is rejected.

## Portfolio policy

Every product receives a lifecycle state:

- candidate
- validated
- building
- launched
- optimizing
- winner
- declining
- archived

The portfolio manager should redirect effort toward products with superior measured economics rather than preserving ideas because of sunk effort.

## Owner-time target

After setup, the system should aim for approximately 15–30 minutes of owner attention per week. Any recurring task that cannot be automated should be treated as technical debt.

## Sale-readiness

The system must preserve:

- clean source control;
- documented product ownership and rights;
- reproducible product-generation procedures;
- marketplace/account boundaries;
- dated research and decision records;
- unit economics and performance history;
- minimal owner dependence;
- clear automation architecture.
